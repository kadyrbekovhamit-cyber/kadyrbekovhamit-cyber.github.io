How to verify AI-assisted research

Use AI to widen the search and compress the reading. Use a deterministic audit to decide what survives.

AI research tools are strong at generating search terms, translating terminology and comparing many documents. They are also capable of inventing a source, merging two findings or quoting a conclusion outside its scope. Verification must be a separate stage with a visible record.

Freeze the exact question

Write the intended use, jurisdiction or population, relevant dates, source types and excluded sources. “Research the market” produces a pile of facts. “Estimate the 2025 addressable market for a defined service in three named US states using primary public sources” creates a checkable task.

Require a source identity

Every material claim should have a stable URL, DOI, filing number, statute citation or dataset identifier. Open it. Confirm the title, author or institution, publication date and version. Search snippets are discovery aids, not evidence.

Check the claim at the source

Find the passage, table or field supporting the claim. Record enough surrounding context to see qualifications. Distinguish what the source observed from what its authors inferred. If a number was calculated from several fields, preserve the formula and inputs.

Recompute fragile numbers

Ratios, percentage changes, currency conversions and unit conversions are cheap to verify and often decisive. Check whether a percentage is relative or absolute, whether values are nominal or real, and whether periods and denominators match.

Search for the strongest contradiction

A report that only confirms its opening thesis is not finished. Search the opposite claim, look for later versions, corrections, failed replications and sources from stakeholders with different incentives. The aim is not artificial balance; it is resistance to selective evidence.

Two-model review is not independence. If both models rely on the same web sources or repeat a common summary, they can agree and still be wrong. Independence comes from evidence and method, not brand names.

Label epistemic status

  • Verified: directly supported and independently checked.
  • Derived: computed from verified inputs.
  • Inferred: a reasoned interpretation with alternatives.
  • Unresolved: evidence is missing or contradictory.

Deliver an audit trail

A client should receive not only prose but a source table, calculation notes, open questions and a record of material corrections. This is especially important when AI assisted the work: auditability is the answer to opacity.

Before delivery
  • open every material source;
  • verify title, date and scope;
  • recompute critical numbers;
  • find at least one serious counter-source;
  • remove or label unsupported claims;
  • state where human judgment entered.

Speed is valuable only after the verification boundary is explicit. The best research workflow is not the one that produces the most text; it is the one that makes errors easy to find.
