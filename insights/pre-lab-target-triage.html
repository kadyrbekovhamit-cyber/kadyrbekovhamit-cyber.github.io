Pre-lab target triage: find the cheapest decisive experiment

The goal is not to prove efficacy from documents. It is to find which assumption deserves laboratory money—and which cheap test can kill it first.

Target selection can accumulate attractive facts without producing a causal chain. Expression, essentiality, binding and disease association are different claims. A pre-lab audit should connect them explicitly and expose the weakest bridge.

Map six questions

  1. Relevance: is the target present and important in the intended biological state?
  2. Mechanism: does modulation plausibly produce the desired phenotype?
  3. Selectivity: what host or off-target systems could be affected?
  4. Access: can an intervention reach the target at a useful exposure?
  5. Resistance or compensation: how might the system route around intervention?
  6. Evidence quality: which claims rest on direct experiments and which on analogy?

Normalize assay conditions before comparing numbers

Measurements from different laboratories may use different pH, temperature, substrates, cofactors, constructs and endpoints. A large apparent selectivity ratio can sometimes be generated by an assay-condition difference rather than an enzyme pocket. Calculate whether a plausible condition shift is large enough to explain the observation before constructing a biological story.

Separate target validity from intervention feasibility

A genetically essential target may still be difficult to drug. Conversely, a compound phenotype does not prove that the nominated target caused it. Keep genetic, biochemical, cellular and in-vivo evidence in separate columns, then state the bridge required between them.

Record contradictions as assets

A failed knockout, condition-dependent phenotype or unexpected tolerance result narrows the mechanism. Do not bury it in “limitations.” Ask which branch of the causal chain it challenges and whether a simple discriminating experiment exists.

Deliverable, not verdict: pre-lab triage does not establish clinical efficacy or replace wet-lab work. It prioritizes uncertainties and designs a cheaper sequence of tests.

Choose the minimum falsifying experiment

For each critical assumption, specify a result threshold that would stop the program or force a redesign. Prefer an experiment that distinguishes competing explanations over one that merely produces another correlated signal.

Decision report
  • claim graph from target to intended outcome;
  • source and condition table;
  • selectivity and access constraints;
  • contradictory evidence;
  • cheapest falsifier with a stopping threshold;
  • what remains unknown after that experiment.

The value is created before the expensive study: not by predicting success with confidence, but by making failure informative and early.
