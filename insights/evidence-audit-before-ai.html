Run an evidence audit before you build with AI

A fluent answer is not the same thing as a supported answer. Before automation, separate what is known from what is merely convenient to assume.

Teams often begin an AI project with a model choice. That is usually too early. The first decision should be whether the evidence can support the action the product is expected to recommend. A model can summarize weak evidence faster; it cannot make weak evidence strong.

Start with one operational claim

Write the claim in a form that could be wrong. “This target looks promising” is not testable. “Under condition X, intervention Y should change measured outcome Z by at least a stated threshold” is much better. Name the population, date range, environment and decision that depends on the claim.

Build five separate lists

  1. Direct evidence: sources that measure the claimed relationship.
  2. Supporting context: relevant sources that do not test the claim directly.
  3. Assumptions: bridges required to move from evidence to action.
  4. Contradictions: results that point in another direction, including failures and boundary cases.
  5. Unknowns: facts the decision needs but the available record does not answer.

Do not allow a citation to move automatically into “direct evidence.” Check what was actually measured, at what time, under which conditions and against which comparator. A source can be credible and still be irrelevant to the precise claim.

Test the direction and the units

Many costly mistakes are not deep. They are sign errors, reversed comparisons, mismatched units or assay conditions treated as biological effects. Recompute simple ratios. Write the expected direction in words. If two studies disagree, compare pH, temperature, dose, endpoint and timing before inventing a mechanistic explanation.

Useful stopping rule: if a plausible measurement or condition confound can explain the full reported effect, do not spend on a more elaborate explanation until the confound is tested.

Name the cheapest falsifier

The output of an evidence audit is not “confidence: 73%.” It is a next action. Ask which affordable observation would most clearly kill the idea. This may be a control experiment, a missing subgroup analysis, an independent replication or a direct question to the source owner.

Keep the audit reproducible

Record source URLs or identifiers, access dates, extracted passages, calculations and the reason each source was assigned to a category. Another person should be able to reproduce the chain without relying on the original analyst's memory.

Minimum deliverable
  • one falsifiable claim;
  • a source table with scope and dates;
  • explicit assumptions and contradictions;
  • recomputed critical numbers;
  • the cheapest decisive next test.

AI is useful inside this workflow: it can search, normalize terminology, compare documents and surface contradictions. The judgment about relevance and the final decision remain accountable human work.
