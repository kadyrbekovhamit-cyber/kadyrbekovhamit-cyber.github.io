Cost conventions and formula definitions

Deposits, annual: contribution = average balance × (net FTP credit − paid interest − variable servicing rate) / 100 − annual fixed servicing cost. The threshold solves the stated contribution target. FTP credit is assumed net of the bank's liquidity, reserve and deposit-insurance allocations and excludes the separate servicing inputs. Deposit capital and other unallocated overheads are outside this specimen.

Loans, annual: contribution before capital = average exposure × (expected recognised interest yield + net earned fee yield − FTP charge − incremental annual credit cost − variable operating rate) / 100 − annual fixed operating cost. Economic surplus then subtracts the stated incremental capital charge. FTP applies to the full stated average exposure. Capital charge must exclude any capital-funding amount already allocated through FTP.

Interest yield excludes fees and already reflects the bank's nonaccrual/foregone-interest treatment. The credit-cost input must not deduct that same loss twice. It is an annualised incremental expense assumption, not a loss-allowance stock or a lifetime ECL amount. Net fee yield excludes passed-through third-party charges; related costs must not also be included in operating inputs. The bank must validate all these conventions before using actual data.

Tariffs, monthly: customer bill = monthly plan fee + max(transactions − included transactions, 0) × per-use fee. Contribution = sum of cohort account counts × (customer bill − processing cost × usage − servicing per account) − monthly platform cost. Refunds, indirect taxes and third-party pass-throughs are outside the fictional fee convention. Every plan uses the same fixed population, so comparisons are not an adoption or switching forecast.

All rate inputs are annual percentages, with up to four decimal places. Stress changes use percentage points. USD inputs use cents; counts are whole nonnegative values. Monetary arithmetic uses integers and retains sub-cent rate effects until display. Individually rounded components may differ from the displayed sum; exact contribution and target-gap amounts are retained in the calculation record. Deposit ceilings round down and loan yield floors round up on a 0.0001 percentage-point grid. A negative or unsupported threshold is shown, not turned into a valid customer offer. Candidate and stressed rates must remain within the specimen's −100% to 100% range; this is a calculation bound, not a legal pricing limit.

Evidence, validation and approval boundaries

Thirteen automated test cases passed on 10 September 2026, including independent reviewer examples, signed threshold rounding, unsupported candidate/stress rates, tariff usage boundaries, exact cents, missing inputs and sub-cent shortfalls. The calculation source also received an independent read-only review. These checks validate the stated specimen mechanics, not a bank's inputs, regulatory approval or realised profitability.

The general FTP principle is supported by the Federal Reserve's published funds-transfer-pricing guidance. It explains allocation of funding and liquidity risks to products. Its stated US institutional applicability is not claimed for an Uzbekistan bank.

Customer APR/APY, amortisation schedules, prepayment, deposit runoff, yield-curve repricing, portfolio interactions, taxes and regulatory capital requirements require a separate bank-approved model. These outputs are neither bank-wide NIM nor accounting net profit. Product, treasury, risk, finance, compliance and operational owners retain approval responsibility.
